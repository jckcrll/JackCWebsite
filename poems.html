Poems by Jack Carlyle

Sometimes I watch the sky and wonder How these puzzle pieces fit With edges fluid as the blood that runs through me The mechanics of the horizon Mysteriously winding around a molten ferrous core Neither slack nor taught Without a need for reprieve, respite, release Tumbling through time

Separating Bifurcate as I tumble Hoping to hoop the moon Swooning from exposure Decomposing as a distant rumble Signals that all will soon Dilute, degrade Laid waste to the late grace Hollow now Unfollowed Quiet Still, Still.

You said your body is not your home I understand the continental drift In time, the fault lines show But our edges then lift Peaks kiss the stars My compass is true Glacial sparks I occupy you
